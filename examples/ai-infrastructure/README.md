# AI infrastructure: test asset life and power commitments together

**Prepared 16 October 2026 | Original analytical handoff | Issuer transition assessment: NEEDS_DATA.**

The [financial case](https://github.com/HHFinAi/Sustainability-to-Financial-Materiality-Analysis/tree/main/examples/ai-infrastructure) compares grid service and hypothetical dedicated gas generation for the same workload. Its purpose is to expose delivery and capital-recovery conditions. It does not establish that Microsoft has selected the modeled gas route, that a particular asset is stranded or that a transition target is credible.

## Separate the clocks

| Clock | Evidence to obtain | How it affects the assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Customer demand and contract term | Incremental workload, minimum commitments, repricing and termination rights | Establish the period and amount of recoverable cash flow |
| Hardware economic service | Cohort performance, replacement plans, redeployment and market resale | Determine cash replacement needs and recoverable value |
| Accounting and tax lives | Book policy, actual tax basis, allowance entitlement and disposal treatment | Reconcile earnings and cash tax; neither proves economic life |
| Power and fuel commitments | Supply term, take-or-pay obligations, standby service, ownership and exit rights | Test mismatch between generation obligations and computing demand |
| Permission and transition requirements | Applicable permits, emissions conditions and dated policy scope | Translate supported obligations into costs or operating constraints |

Microsoft's FY2026 filing supplies consolidated financial and accounting context, not GPU-specific economic lives or site tax entitlement. Its Wisconsin and Atlanta disclosures describe distinct power/design arrangements. Source IDs **MS-10K-FY2026**, **MS-WI-DESIGN-20250918** and **MS-ATL-20251112** are in the [primary register](https://github.com/HHFinAi/Sustainability-to-Financial-Materiality-Analysis/blob/main/examples/ai-infrastructure/evidence/sources.json).

## Apply the model with its limits

The model separates hardware economic replacement from tax allowances and tests replacement lives, carbon-cost assumptions and recovery values. All such inputs are assumptions. The common 120-month opportunity window can change replacement counts abruptly as commissioning moves; inspect the [methods and terminal-boundary challenge](https://github.com/HHFinAi/Sustainability-to-Financial-Materiality-Analysis/blob/main/examples/ai-infrastructure/METHODS.md) before interpreting route rankings.

A longer-lived generation asset supporting shorter-lived hardware is a question about utilization, reuse and contracts. It is not proof of unavoidable loss. Conversely, a positive reference NPV does not demonstrate compatibility with an issuer's transition target.

## Evidence needed for credibility

1. Reconcile generation ownership, purchased electricity and the issuer's emissions inventory boundaries. Avoid treating a scope transfer as physical emissions reduction.
2. Obtain the target baseline, covered emissions, interim trajectory and actual implementation funding. Keep absolute emissions, intensity and workload growth distinct.
3. Test delivered alternatives with consistent reliability, schedule, capacity, cost and emissions boundaries. Do not assume storage duration, grid availability or fuel delivery.
4. Compare asset and contract commitments with the transition pathway, including credible reuse, retrofit, retirement and residual-value evidence.
5. Record observed implementation milestones and explain unresolved dependencies before any issuer credibility conclusion.
